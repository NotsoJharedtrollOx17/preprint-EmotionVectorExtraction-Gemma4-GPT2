\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Story Generation}

Following the prompting strategy of \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026}, we used a modified template
(Appendix~\ref{apdx:story-context}) to generate emotionally expressive narratives. For each category, Google's Gemini model
produced batches of five short stories for each of ten topics in JSON format, allowing the outputs to be organized by emotion.
We retained the stories as generated, without manual rewriting or post-hoc quality filtering, to preserve a simple first-pass
corpus for this partial replication. A separate template (Appendix~\ref{apdx:neutral-prompt}) produced emotionally neutral
commands and statements for the intermediate neutral-centering calculation described below. The reported analyses therefore
characterize this corpus-and-pipeline combination and do not establish independence from corpus artifacts.

\subsection{Emotion Vector Extraction}

\paragraph{Hidden Representation Extraction} Each story is passed through the frozen language model to obtain the hidden
activations at a selected Transformer depth:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_i^{(l)} = f_\theta^{(l)}(x_i)
    \label{eqtn:hidden-representation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)}$ denotes the layer-$l$ hidden-state sequence for story $x_i\in\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}$ under
the forward computation $f_\theta^{(l)}$. We use the layer closest to two-thirds of each model's depth, following the mid-late-layer convention reported 
by \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026}. This fixed rule selects numeric index 16 for GPT-2 Medium and index 23 for Gemma 4 E2B; it is a source-informed 
transfer choice, not a claim that these locations are optimal for either model. In the implementation, extraction uses \texttt{outputs.hidden\_states[$l$]}, 
whereas steering hooks the transformer block at index $l$. Because the Hugging Face hidden-state tuple includes the embedding output at index zero, 
these two interfaces may refer to adjacent residual-stream locations; we retain this implemented indexing convention and treat it as a limitation.
We repeat the extraction for every story in each emotion-specific set $\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}$ and for every
$\mathtt{emotion}\in\mathcal{E}$. The label set $\mathcal{E}$ contains either nine or twenty emotions, listed in
Appendix~\ref{apdx:full-emotion-list}.

\paragraph{Token Aggregation} Since every generated story has a different length, the extracted token representations are averaged. Let $L_i$ be 
the number of non-padding tokens in story $i$. The implemented starting index is $t_{0,i}=50$ when $L_i>50$ and $t_{0,i}=\lfloor L_i/2\rfloor$ otherwise. 
Using zero-based token indices, the pooled representation is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\overline{h}}_i=\frac{1}{L_i-t_{0,i}}\sum_{t=t_{0,i}}^{L_i-1}\mathbf{h}_{i,t}^{(l)}
    \label{eqtn:token-aggr}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{\overline{h}}_i$ is the pooled representation of story $i$ and $\mathbf{h}_{i,t}^{(l)}$ is its token-$t$ hidden representation at 
the selected extraction index.

\paragraph{Prototype Computation} We average the pooled story representations within each emotion category:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathtt{emotion}}\right|}\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}\right|}\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{emotion},i}
    \label{eqtn:prototype-comp}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the emotion prototype,
$\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{emotion},i}$ is the pooled representation of story $i$, and
$\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathtt{emotion}}\right|$ is the number of stories in that category.

\paragraph{Mean Neutral Statements} Following our interpretation of the extraction procedure described by Sofroniew et al.\ \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026} and the open-weight replication by Hsieh \cite{hsieh_replicating_2026}, we first compute the mean neutral representation from the hidden activations obtained from the neutral-statements dataset:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{neutral}}=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{N}\right|}\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\mathcal{N}\right|}\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{neutral},i}
    \label{eqtn:neutral-mean}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{neutral}}$ is the mean neutral representation, $\left|\mathcal{N}\right|$ is the number of neutral prompts, and
$\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{neutral},i}$ is the mean hidden representation of neutral prompt $i$. We then form a neutral-adjusted prototype 
for each emotion:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\nu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{neutral}}
    \label{eqtn:neutral-adjusted-prototype}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Global Mean Centering} We center each neutral-adjusted prototype by subtracting their global mean. This mean is:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\nu}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{{\mathtt{emotion}}\in \mathcal{E}}^{}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}
    \label{eqtn:global-mean}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\nu}$ is the global mean of the neutral-adjusted emotion prototypes.

We calculate the centered emotion vector as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}-\boldsymbol{\nu}
    \label{eqtn:emotion-vector}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the globally centered, neutral-adjusted prototype. Because the same neutral mean is subtracted from 
every emotion prototype, it cancels during global centering: $\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_e$. 
Thus, the final vectors are globally emotion-centered; the neutral corpus is retained only in the intermediate calculation.

\paragraph{Vector Normalization} We L2-normalize each centered direction:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\frac{\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}}{\lVert \mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}} \rVert_2}
    \label{eqtn:normalized-emotion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the normalized emotion vector and $\lVert \mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}} \rVert_2$ is the Euclidean
(L2) norm of the unnormalized vector $\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$.

\subsection{Logit Lens Extraction}

Following the diagnostic used by \cite{hsieh_replicating_2026}, we project each normalized direction into vocabulary space with a direct 
Logit Lens. This projection identifies tokens aligned with the direction under the model's output head; it is not treated as a reconstruction 
of an intermediate next-token distribution.

\paragraph{Initial Projection} The implementation first applies the model's final normalization $\mathcal{N}_f$ and then its language-model head:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=W_U\mathcal{N}_f\!\left(\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}\right)+\mathbf{b}_U
    \label{eqtn:initial-projection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the list of raw logit values for $\mathtt{emotion}$. $W_U$ 
is the learned unembedding matrix and $\mathbf{b}_U$ is the learned output-head bias when the architecture provides one (and is zero otherwise). 
No random-noise bias is added.

The resulting vector contains one value per vocabulary item:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=[z_1,z_2,\dots,z_{|V|}]
    \label{eqtn:logits-set}
\end{equation}
where $z_t$ is the raw score for token $t$ and $|V|$ is the vocabulary size.

\paragraph{Vocabulary Standardization} To rank vocabulary items on a common scale within each projected direction, we standardize the raw 
logits into $z$-scores.
\begin{equation}
    \mu_z=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{t\in V}^{} z_t
    \label{eqtn:mean-logits}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_z$ is the mean raw score over vocabulary $V$.

We then compute the sample standard deviation:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_z=\sqrt{\frac{1}{|V|-1}\sum_{t\in V}^{}\left(z_t-\mu_z\right)^2}
    \label{eqtn:stddev-logits}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_z$ is the standard deviation of the list of raw logit scores $z_t$.

Finally, we compute the z-score of each vocabulary token $t$ with:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{z}_t=\frac{z_t-\mu_z}{\sigma_z}
    \label{eqtn:bell-logits}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{z}_t$ is the z-score of token $t$.

\paragraph{Top- and Bottom-k Vocabulary Extraction} We select the highest- and lowest-scoring vocabulary items:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{T}_k=\text{TopK}(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}, k)
    \label{eqtn:topk-vocabs}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{B}_k=\text{BottomK}(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}, k)
    \label{eqtn:bottomk-vocabs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_k$ is the set of top $k$ z-scored tokens, $\mathcal{B}_k$ is the set of 
bottom $k$ z-scored tokens, and $k$ is the number of tokens to extract from the set 
$\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$.

\subsection{Emotion Probe Steering}

To perform the steering experiments, we modify the hooked block output at each token position using:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{t,\mathtt{emotion}}^{(l)}=\mathbf{h}_{t}^{(l)}+\alpha\left\lVert\mathbf{h}_{t}^{(l)}\right\rVert_2\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}
    \label{eqtn:emotion-steering}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{t,\mathtt{emotion}}^{(l)}$ is the emotion-steered hidden activation at token position $t$ and layer $l$, 
and $\alpha$ is the steering coefficient. We set $\alpha=+0.5$ because \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026} reports steering results at 
strength 0.5. Anthropic defines that strength relative to an average residual-stream norm over a large dataset; our implementation 
instead scales each intervention by the current token's residual norm. Thus, $\alpha=0.5$ is a source-informed operating point rather 
than a numerically identical reproduction of Anthropic's intervention strength, and we do not interpret it as an optimized or 
cross-model-comparable setting.

\subsection{Delta Log Probability}

For a fixed prompt $q$, we compare baseline and steered next-token log probabilities. For steering direction $e$, coefficient
$\alpha$, and target token $w$, the change is:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta_e(w\mid q,\alpha)=\log p_{e,\alpha}(w\mid q)-\log p_0(w\mid q)
    \label{eqtn:deltalog-prob}
\end{equation}
where $p_0$ and $p_{e,\alpha}$ are the softmax distributions at the final prompt position without and with steering. Let $\mathcal{T}_j$ be 
the five top Logit-Lens tokens for target emotion $j$. The plotted heatmap entry is
\begin{equation}
    H_{e,j}(q,\alpha)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_j|}\sum_{w\in\mathcal{T}_j}\Delta_e(w\mid q,\alpha),
    \label{eqtn:deltalog-heatmap}
\end{equation}
evaluated at $\alpha=0.5$. Because $\mathcal{T}_j$ is derived from the same vectors used for steering, this is a direction-consistency 
diagnostic rather than an independent semantic test. When a displayed token string re-encodes to multiple token IDs, the implementation 
sums their log probabilities at the same next-token position; this is an approximation, not an autoregressive multi-token sequence probability.

\subsection{PCA Projection}
We compute a two-dimensional PCA projection with \texttt{scikit-learn}. The components are estimated without emotion ratings; any valence- or 
arousal-like interpretation is therefore made descriptively after inspecting the labeled projection and is not built into the PCA calculation.

\paragraph{Emotion Matrix} We stack the normalized emotion vectors by row:
\begin{equation}
    X=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{\hat{v}}_1 \\
        \mathbf{\hat{v}}_2 \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf{\hat{v}}_{|\mathcal{E}|}
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eqtn:pca-emotionMatrix}
\end{equation}
where $X$ is the matrix of normalized emotion vectors and $|\mathcal{E}|$ is either nine or twenty.

\paragraph{Matrix Centering} Before decomposition, we center each feature across emotion vectors:
\begin{equation}
    X_c = X - \bar{X}
    \label{eqtn:pca-centering}
\end{equation}
where $X_c$ is the centered emotion matrix and $\bar{X}$ is the feature-wise mean vector.

\paragraph{Singular Value Decomposition} We decompose the centered matrix as:
\begin{equation}
    X_c=U\Sigma V^{\top}
    \label{eqtn:pca-svd}
\end{equation}
where $U$ contains the left singular vectors, $\Sigma$ contains the singular values, and $V^{\top}$ contains the
principal-component directions in the original hidden-state space.

\paragraph{Projection of 2D Coordinates} We project onto the first two principal-component directions:
\begin{equation}
    Y = X_c V_{:,1:2}
    \label{eqtn:pca-proj}
\end{equation}
where $Y$ contains the plotted two-dimensional coordinates and $V_{:,1:2}$ contains the first two right singular vectors.

\subsection{Cosine Similarity}

Finally, we compute pairwise cosine similarity to summarize directional alignment between emotion vectors. The resulting
matrix is displayed as a heatmap:
\begin{equation}
    S_{i,j}=\frac{\mathbf{\hat{v}}_i\cdot \mathbf{\hat{v}}_j}{\lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_i \rVert \lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_j \rVert}
    \label{eqtn:cos-similarity}
\end{equation}
where $S_{i,j}$ is the cosine similarity score between emotion vectors $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_i$ and $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_j$.

Because our emotion vectors are normalized, we note that $\lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_i \rVert=\lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_j \rVert=1$. 
Therefore $S_{i,j}$ can be simplified to the following expression:
\begin{equation}
    S_{i,j}=\mathbf{\hat{v}}_i\cdot \mathbf{\hat{v}_j}
    \label{eqtn:cos-sim-reduced}
\end{equation}
