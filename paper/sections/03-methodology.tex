\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Story Generation}

Following the prompting strategy proposed by \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026}, we employed a slightly modified prompt template (Appendix ~\ref{apdx:story-context})
to generate emotionally expressive narratives. For each emotion category, Google's Gemini language model was instructed to 
produce batches of 5 short stories following one of 10 story topics as a JSON array, facilitating their organization into 
emotion-specific datasets. In addition, a separate neutral prompt template (Appendix ~\ref{apdx:neutral-prompt}) was used to generate a 
collection of emotionally neutral commands and requests, which served as the reference corpus for the intermediate neutral-centering step described below.

\subsection{Emotion Vector Extraction}

%\subsubsection{Hidden Representation Extraction}
\paragraph{Hidden Representation Extraction} Each generated emotional story is first propagated through the frozen language model state to obtain the hidden activation of a selected 
transformer layer. This is computed with the following equation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{h}_i^{(l)} = f_\theta^{(l)}(x_i)
    \label{eqtn:hidden-representation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{h}_i^{(l)}$ is the hidden state activation obtained from story $x_i\in\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}$ inside the forward computation
$f_\theta^{(l)}$ on layer $l$, where $l$ is considered to be $\sim\nicefrac{2}{3}$ layer depth for each LLM. This is calculated on each 
$\mathtt{emotion}\in\mathcal{E}$, for each emotion story inside the story dataset $\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}$. Furthermore, $\mathcal{E}$ 
can be either the 9-emotion, or 20-emotion, dataset list available on Appendix~\ref{apdx:full-emotion-list}. Finally, $\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}$ 
is the set of generated emotional stories that convey the emotional tone of \texttt{emotion}.

%\subsubsection{Token Aggregation}
\paragraph{Token Aggregation} Since every generated story has a different length, the token representations previously extracted are averaged.
This averaging follows the equation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\overline{h}}_i=\frac{1}{T_i}\sum_{t=t_0}^{T_i}\mathbf{h}_{i,t}^{(l)}
    \label{eqtn:token-aggr}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{\overline{h}}_i$ is the mean hidden representation of the complete story $i$, while $\mathbf{h}_{i,t}^{(l)}$
represents the hidden representation of each token $t$ inside $i$. Finally, $T_i$ is the number of generated tokens
from story $i$. 

%\subsubsection{Prototype Computation}
\paragraph{Prototype Computation} Stories belonging to the same emotion are averaged to better represent the overarching emotion. Said calculation follows the equation:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathtt{emotion}}\right|}\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\mathcal{S}_\mathtt{emotion}\right|}\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{emotion},i}
    \label{eqtn:prototype-comp}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the mean hidden activation of all stories $i$ conveying 
the tone of \texttt{emotion}. On a similar vein, $\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{emotion},i}$ represents the mean individual activation 
for story $i$ belonging to \texttt{emotion}. Finally, $\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathtt{emotion}}\right|$ is the total number of emotionally
expressive stories for \texttt{emotion}.

%\subsubsection{Mean Neutral Statements}
\paragraph{Mean Neutral Statements} Following our interpretation of the extraction procedure described by Sofroniew et al.\ \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026} and the open-weight replication by Hsieh \cite{hsieh_replicating_2026}, we first compute the mean neutral representation from the hidden activations obtained from the neutral-statements dataset:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{neutral}}=\frac{1}{\left|\mathcal{N}\right|}\sum_{i=1}^{\left|\mathcal{N}\right|}\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{neutral},i}
    \label{eqtn:neutral-mean}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{neutral}}$ is the mean neutral representation, $\left|\mathcal{N}\right|$ is the number of neutral prompts, and
$\mathbf{\overline{h}}_{\mathtt{neutral},i}$ is the mean hidden representation of neutral prompt $i$. We then form a neutral-adjusted prototype for each emotion:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\nu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}-\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{neutral}}
    \label{eqtn:neutral-adjusted-prototype}
\end{equation}

%\subsubsection{Global Mean Centering}
\paragraph{Global Mean Centering} We center each neutral-adjusted prototype by subtracting their global mean. This mean is:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\nu}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{{\mathtt{emotion}}\in \mathcal{E}}^{}\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}
    \label{eqtn:global-mean}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{\nu}$ is the global mean of the neutral-adjusted emotion prototypes.

We calculate the centered emotion vector as follows:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\boldsymbol{\nu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}-\boldsymbol{\nu}
    \label{eqtn:emotion-vector}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the globally centered, neutral-adjusted prototype. Because the same neutral mean is subtracted from every emotion prototype, it cancels during global centering: $\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{\mathtt{emotion}}-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}|}\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\boldsymbol{\mu}_e$. Thus, the final vectors are globally emotion-centered; the neutral corpus is retained only in the intermediate calculation.

%\subsubsection{Vector Normalization}
\paragraph{Vector Normalization} Finally, for computational convenience and numerical stability, we normalize the emotion vector by performing the following
computation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=\frac{\mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}}}{\lVert \mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}} \rVert_2}
    \label{eqtn:normalized-emotion}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the normalized emotion vector and $\lVert \mathbf{v}_{\mathtt{emotion}} \rVert_2$ is the Euclidean
(L2) norm of $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$.

\subsection{Logit Lens Extraction}

Taking inspiration from the replication attempt by \cite{hsieh_replicating_2026}, the following equations
illustrate the extraction of the vocabulary tokens, also known as logit lents in the 
realms of Mechanistic Interpretability.

%\subsubsection{Initial Projection}
\paragraph{Initial Projection} First and foremost, we extract the list of raw logit values from the emotion vector $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ 
with the following equation:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=W_U\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}+\mathbf{b}
    \label{eqtn:initial-projection}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ is the list of raw logit values for $\mathtt{emotion}$. $W_U$ 
is the weight matrix of the unembedding layer of the transformer, which captures the search space 
of representative tokens. Finally, $\mathbf{b}$ is the bias vector coming from a forward pass of 
random noise from previous layers of the model.

Expanding the results, $\mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ contains the following list of values:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}=[z_1,z_2,\dots,z_{|V|}]
    \label{eqtn:logits-set}
\end{equation}
where $z_t$ is the raw logit value of token $t$, while $|V|$ is the total number of tokens in 
vocabulary $V$.

%\subsubsection{Vocabulary Standarization}
\paragraph{Vocabulary Standarization} To facilitate the logit lens interpretation, we translate the raw logit scores into standardized
z-scores. This tells us which logits are unlikely to be near the mean of the distribution, and 
henceforth representative of \texttt{emotion}.
\begin{equation}
    \mu_z=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{t\in V}^{} z_t
    \label{eqtn:mean-logits}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_z$ is the mean of the raw logit scores $z_t$, where $t$ is a token of the vocabulary $V$.
Finally, $|V|$ is the total number of tokens in the vocabulary.

Next, we compute the standard deviation of the raw logit scores with the following formula:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_z=\sqrt{\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{t\in V}^{}\left(z_t-\mu_z\right)^2}
    \label{eqtn:stddev-logits}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma_z$ is the standard deviation of the list of raw logit scores $z_t$.

Finally, we compute the z-score of each vocabulary token $t$ with:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{z}_t=\frac{z_t-\mu_z}{\sigma_z}
    \label{eqtn:bell-logits}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{z}_t$ is the z-score of token $t$.

%\subsubsection{Top- and Bottom-k Vocabulary Extraction}
\paragraph{Top- and Bottom-k Vocabulary Extraction} As a selection criterion for the extraction process, we utilize:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{T}_k=\text{TopK}(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}, k)
    \label{eqtn:topk-vocabs}
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{B}_k=\text{BottomK}(\tilde{\mathbf{z}}, k)
    \label{eqtn:bottomk-vocabs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_k$ is the set of top $k$ z-scored tokens, $\mathcal{B}_k$ is the set of 
bottom $k$ z-scored tokens, and $k$ is the number of tokens to extract from the set 
$\tilde{\mathbf{z}}$.

\subsection{Emotion Probe Steering}

To perform the causal steering experiments, we utilize the following equation:
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}^{(l)}=\mathbf{h}^{(l)}+\alpha\cdot\mathbf{\hat{v}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}
    \label{eqtn:emotion-steering}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathtt{emotion}}^{(l)}$ is the extracted \emph{emotion-steered} hidden activation 
at layer $l$. Furthermore, $\alpha$ is considered as the steering factor, which we set to the number +0.5 
throughout the experiments.

\subsection{Delta Log Probability}

To determine the strength of the steering intervention, we calculate the logit lens for both the baseline
response and the steered response, we then apply the $\log$ operation to both results, and finally calculate
the difference of the steered response and the baseline response.
\begin{equation}
    \Delta=\log p_\text{steered}(\mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}})-\log p_\text{baseline}(\mathbf{z})
    \label{eqtn:deltalog-prob}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta$ is the result of the difference. $\log p_\text{steered}(\mathtt{emotion})$ is the log-scaled logit score of 
the steered response tokens $\mathbf{z}_{\mathtt{emotion}}$ and $\log p_\text{baseline}(\mathbf{z})$ is the log-scaled 
logit score of the baseline response tokens $\mathbf{z}$.

\subsection{PCA Projection}
To project the Valence-Arousal PCA plot, we compute PCA with the \texttt{scikit-learn} library, which performs 
the following calculations.

%\subsubsection{Emotion Matrix}
\paragraph{Emotion Matrix} First and foremost, we construct the matrix containing the normalized emotion vectors,
\begin{equation}
    X=
    \begin{bmatrix}
        \mathbf{\hat{v}}_1 \\
        \mathbf{\hat{v}}_2 \\
        \vdots \\
        \mathbf{\hat{v}}_{|\mathcal{E}|}
    \end{bmatrix}
    \label{eqtn:pca-emotionMatrix}
\end{equation}
where $X$ is the matrix whose rows are normalized emotion vectors. $|\mathcal{E}|$ is total number of emotions
utilized, which can either be 9 emotions, or 20 emotions total.

%\subsubsection{Matrix Centering}
\paragraph{Matrix Centering} Before the actual decomposition, we center every feature by subtracting the global mean of all vectors:
\begin{equation}
    X_c = X - \bar{X}
    \label{eqtn:pca-centering}
\end{equation}
where $X_c$ is the centered emotion matrix used internally during the PCA computation and $\bar{X}$ is the 
aforementioned global mean vector.

%\subsubsection{Singular Value Decomposition}
\paragraph{Singular Value Decomposition} Now, the singular value decomposition is computed by decomposing $X_c$ into the following expressions:
\begin{equation}
    X_c=U\Sigma V^{\top}
    \label{eqtn:pca-svd}
\end{equation}
where $U$ are the left singular vectors describing each emotion sample. $\Sigma$ is the diagonal matrix containing 
singular values. Finally, $V^{\top}$ represents the principal components in the original hidden-state space.

%\subsubsection{Projection of 2D Coordinates}
\paragraph{Projection of 2D Coordinates} Finally, we perform the projection onto the first two principal components of the previously extracted $V^{\top}$:
\begin{equation}
    Y = X_c V_{1:2}
    \label{eqtn:pca-proj}
\end{equation}
where $Y$ is the two-dimensional PCA coordinates utilized in the PCA plot, and $V_{1:2}$ is the matrix formed by the
\emph{first two directions} of the principal components.

\subsection{Cosine Similarity}

Our final experiment, cosine similarity, is performed to calculate similarities, and differences, between
emotion vectors. The computed scores are further illustrated inside a heatmap which visualizes emotion 
clusters. To calculate this, we utilize the following equation:
\begin{equation}
    S_{i,j}=\frac{\mathbf{\hat{v}}_i\cdot \mathbf{\hat{v}_j}}{\lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_i \rVert \lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_j \rVert}
    \label{eqtn:cos-similarity}
\end{equation}
where $S_{i,j}$ is the cosine similarity score between emotion vectors $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_i$ and $\mathbf{\hat{v}}_j$.

Because our emotion vectors are normalized, we note that $\lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_i \rVert=\lVert \mathbf{\hat{v}}_j \rVert=1$. 
Therefore $S_{i,j}$ can be simplified to the following expression:
\begin{equation}
    S_{i,j}=\mathbf{\hat{v}}_i\cdot \mathbf{\hat{v}_j}
    \label{eqtn:cos-sim-reduced}
\end{equation}
