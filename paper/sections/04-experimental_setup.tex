\section{Experimental Setup}

The proposed methodology was evaluated on two open-weight transformer language models using emotion
datasets containing either 9 or 20 emotion categories. All experiments follow the same activation 
extraction, steering, and evaluation pipeline described in Section [section citation]. The implementation
was developed in Python via Google Colab notebooks, using the PyTorch, HuggingFace Transformers, and 
scikit-learn libraries.

\subsection{Models}
Two language models were evaluated:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \texttt{openai-community/gpt-2-medium}, a publicly available 355-million-parameter autoregressive Transformer model released by OpenAI, serving as a reproducible baseline representative of early large language models \cite{radford_language_2019}.
    \item \texttt{google/gemma-4-e2b}, a recently released open-weight language model from Google designed for instruction-following and multilingual reasoning \cite{team_gemma_2026}.
\end{itemize}

These models were selected to evaluate whether the proposed methodology generalizes across language models differing substantially in architecture, scale, tokenizer, training corpus, and multilingual capabilities. In particular, 
GPT-2 Medium serves as a historical baseline to investigate whether interpretable emotion representations emerge in earlier-generation Transformer models, whereas Gemma-4 E2B enables evaluation on a modern instruction-tuned architecture. 
Observing consistent behavior across both models would suggest that the extracted emotion vectors reflect general properties of Transformer representations rather than model-specific characteristics.

\subsection{Emotion Datasets}

The proposed methodology was evaluated using two emotion datasets derived from a common emotion-story corpus. The 
first corresponds to the publicly available nine-emotion list adopted by \cite{hsieh_replicating_2026}, providing a reproducible
baseline for comparison. Building upon this benchmark, we additionally construct an extended dataset containing twenty
emotion categories to evaluate the proposed methodology under a broader and finer-grained affective space.

Unlike \cite{sofroniew_emotion_2026}, which investigated 171 emotional concepts, this work intentionally focuses on smaller
emotion datasets that are practical to analyze and compare while remaining computationally reproducible inside free-tier
computing hosts. The twenty-emotion dataset extends the original nine emotions with semantically related affective states, 
resulting in increased emotional granularity and greater overlap between neighboring representations. To ensure a controlled 
comparison, the nine-emotion experiments were conducted using the corresponding subset of the twenty-emotion emotional stories,
allowing differences between both settings to be primarily attributed to the number of modeled emotions categories 
\emph{rather than variations in dataset generation or preprocessing.}

\subsection{Steering Configuration}

The causal steering experiments followed the evaluation protocol proposed by Anthropic. Two neutral prompt templates (Prompt00 and Prompt01) were
employed to elicit unconstrained text continuations before and after applying emotion vector steering. By minimizing the emotional content of 
the initial prompt, the resulting generations provide a controlled setting for observing the causal influence of the injected emotion vectors on
the semantic, lexical, and stylistic characteristics of the generated text output. Throughout all steering experiments, a fixed steering coefficient
of $\alpha=+0.5$ was used. Text was generated using stochastic sampling with a temperature of 0.7 and a maximum generation length of 100 new tokens.
Both prompt templates were evaluated independently for the 9-emotion and 20-emotion datasets across \texttt{openai-community/gpt-2-medium} and 
\texttt{google/gemma-4-e2b}, thereby ensuring identical text generation conditions and allowing direct qualitative comparisons of the emotional
steering behavior induced by each extracted emotion vector. Table ~\ref{tab:steering-config} summarizes the configuration employed throughout 
the experiments.

\subsection{Evaluation Pipeline}

The evaluation followed a fixed pipeline for both \texttt{openai-community/gpt-2-medium} and \texttt{google/gemma-4-e2b}. For each
model, emotion vectors were first extracted independently from the 9-emotion and 20-emotion datasets using the extraction 
configuration described previously. The resulting emotion libraries were then evaluated through four complementary analyses. First,
Logit Lens extraction was performed to identify the vocabulary tokens most strongly associated with each extracted emotion vector. Second,
causal steering experiments were conducted by injecting each emotion vector into the selected transformer layer while generating 
continuations from Prompt00 and Prompt01. The semantic effects of the steering intervention were quantified and visualized through Delta
Log Probability heatmaps, while representative generated excerpts were qualitatively examined to assess whether the steered text outputs
consistently reflected the intended emotional concepts. Furthermore, Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was applied to visualize the global
organization of the learned emotional representation space. Finally, Cosine Similarity heatmaps were plotted to understand the strength of
the similarities and differences of the evaluated emotions.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

All experiments were implemented in Python using Google Colab notebooks. Model inference was performed with the HuggingFace 
\emph{Transformers} library and PyTorch, while activation extraction, residual stream steering, and Logit Lens analyses were
implemented using custom Python scripts. Principal Component Analysis (PCA) was performed with scikit-learn, and quantitative
analyses were conducted using NumPy and Pandas. Visualizations, including PCA projections, Cosine Similarity Heatmaps, and Delta 
Log Probability heatmaps, were generated using Matplotlib and Plotly. To reduce redundant computation, extracted emotion vectors
were cached and reused throughout the evaluation pipeline. Finally, all experiments were executed using the free-tier Google Colab
environment equipped with NVIDIA T4 GPUs.
