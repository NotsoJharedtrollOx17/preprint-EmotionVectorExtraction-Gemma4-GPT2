\section{Results}

We evaluated the extracted vectors in \texttt{openai-community/gpt-2-medium} and \texttt{google/gemma-4-e2b} using four 
descriptive analyses: Logit Lens projections, steering heatmaps and representative continuations, PCA projections, 
and pairwise cosine-similarity heatmaps. Each analysis was run for the nine- and 20-emotion sets. The results below 
report patterns visible in these outputs. In particular, PCA axes are data-dependent and have arbitrary signs; references 
to a ``valence-like'' direction describe the ordering of selected labeled emotions, rather than a validated correspondence 
with human affect ratings.

\subsection{GPT 2 Medium}

\subsubsection{Logit Lens}

For GPT-2 Medium, several projected token lists align directly with their labels. For example, the \texttt{happy}, \texttt{calm}, 
\texttt{loving}, \texttt{angry}, and \texttt{desperate} vectors rank tokens such as \emph{joyful}, \emph{relaxation}, 
\emph{nurturing}, \emph{angrily}, and \emph{frantically}, respectively. These patterns persist in the 20-emotion set, 
where additional examples include \texttt{lonely} (\emph{solitude}, \emph{lonely}) and \texttt{brooding} (\emph{haunted}, 
\emph{melancholy}); see \Cref{tab:gpt2_logitlens_9,tab:gpt2_logitlens_20}. Other rows are less direct---for instance, 
\texttt{surprised} is associated with technical and fragmentary tokens. The Logit Lens tables therefore provide a diagnostic 
of directions in vocabulary space, not an independent validation that every vector uniquely encodes its label.

\subsubsection{Emotion Steering}

The GPT-2 Medium steering heatmaps show that the intervention changes next-token log probabilities under both prompts 
and emotion-set sizes (\Cref{fig:gpt2-deltalogprob}). Each cell is the mean change for the target emotion's five Logit-Lens-derived 
tokens; consequently, the diagonal and related blocks are a direction-consistent diagnostic rather than an independent semantic 
evaluation. The representative continuations show corresponding lexical changes, but they also exhibit frequent repetition and 
degraded fluency---for example, repeated \emph{joy} under \texttt{happy} steering and repeated phrasing under \texttt{loving} 
steering (\Cref{tab:gpt2-excerptSteering}). These outputs establish that the fixed intervention changes GPT-2 Medium's 
generation, while leaving the specificity and text quality of that change unresolved.

\subsubsection{PCA Projection}

For the nine-emotion GPT-2 Medium set, the first two components explain 61.1\% of the variance (PC1: 40.1\%; PC2: 21.0\%). Up to the 
arbitrary sign of PC1, \texttt{happy}, \texttt{calm}, and \texttt{loving} occupy one side, whereas \texttt{afraid}, \texttt{angry}, 
and \texttt{desperate} occupy the other. \texttt{sad}, \texttt{guilty}, and \texttt{surprised} do not follow this ordering cleanly. 
On the displayed axes, \texttt{desperate} and \texttt{guilty} occupy the upper-left quadrant; \texttt{sad}, \texttt{surprised}, and 
\texttt{loving} the upper-right; \texttt{afraid} and \texttt{angry} the lower-left; and \texttt{happy} and \texttt{calm} the lower-right. 
The 20-emotion projection explains 49.5\% in its first two components (35.3\% and 14.2\%), but its PC1 ordering is less uniform across 
the expanded labels. Its upper-right quadrant contains \texttt{calm}, \texttt{happy}, \texttt{sad}, \texttt{loving}, and \texttt{lonely}, 
whereas the lower-left contains \texttt{angry}, \texttt{disgusted}, \texttt{nervous}, and \texttt{confused}; \texttt{surprised}, 
\texttt{playful}, and \texttt{spiteful} are in the lower-right. These quadrant locations are visual diagnostics of the displayed axes, 
whose signs may reverse, rather than a demonstrated valence--arousal coordinate system (\Cref{fig:gpt2-pca-projection}).

\subsubsection{Cosine Similarity Heatmap}

The GPT-2 Medium cosine matrices show several repeated local relationships. In the nine-emotion set, \texttt{afraid}--\texttt{angry} has 
a cosine similarity of 0.76, while \texttt{loving}--\texttt{afraid} has a cosine similarity of -0.78 and \texttt{happy}--\texttt{desperate} 
has one of -0.57. In the 20-emotion set, \texttt{anxious}--\texttt{afraid}, \texttt{afraid}--\texttt{angry}, and \texttt{hopeful}--\texttt{inspired} 
have cosine similarities of 0.92, 0.82, and 0.85, respectively, forming visible high-similarity pairs. Several pairs across these 
groups have negative cosine similarities. These are descriptive directional alignments; they do not establish a human affect taxonomy or 
a one-to-one semantic representation (\Cref{fig:gpt2-cos-sim9,fig:gpt2-cos-sim20}).

\subsubsection{Emotion Steering}

Gemma 4 E2B likewise shows prompt- and direction-dependent changes in the target-token heatmaps (\Cref{fig:gemma-deltalogprob}). 
The representative generations contain label-related words in some conditions, such as \emph{happy}, \emph{great}, and \emph{love}, 
but also include HTML-like markup, repetition, and unrelated dialogue continuations (\Cref{tab:gemma-excerptSteering}). As for 
GPT-2 Medium, these heatmaps measure mean next-token log-probability changes for token sets derived from the same emotion 
vectors used for steering. Several diagonal cells are modest relative to off-diagonal cells. This is not unexpected: a diagonal cell 
tests whether adding a direction at layer 23 increases the five tokens selected by that direction's static Logit Lens projection, but 
the remaining model layers can transform the intervention before the final logits are computed. The five-token averages can also include 
multilingual tokens, fragments, or related rather than exact lexical realizations. Off-diagonal effects may therefore reflect shared 
directional structure rather than failed steering. The heatmaps show that the intervention affects this diagnostic, but do not independently 
quantify emotional control or generation quality.

\subsubsection{PCA Projection}

For the nine-emotion Gemma 4 E2B set, the first two components explain 58.3\% of the variance (PC1: 38.3\%; PC2: 20.0\%). 
Up to the arbitrary sign of PC1, \texttt{happy}, \texttt{calm}, and \texttt{loving} lie on one end, whereas \texttt{afraid}, 
\texttt{angry}, \texttt{desperate}, and \texttt{guilty} lie on the other; \texttt{sad} and \texttt{surprised} are intermediate 
or exception cases. In the displayed coordinate system, \texttt{calm} and \texttt{happy} occupy the upper-left quadrant, \texttt{loving}, 
\texttt{sad}, and \texttt{surprised} the lower-left, \texttt{afraid} and \texttt{angry} the upper-right, and \texttt{guilty} with 
near-horizontal \texttt{desperate} the lower-right. The 20-emotion projection explains 48.4\% in its first two components (32.0\% and 16.4\%), with several 
positive-labeled vectors---including \texttt{hopeful}, \texttt{inspired}, \texttt{proud}, \texttt{happy}, and \texttt{loving}---
separated from a group containing \texttt{anxious}, \texttt{afraid}, and \texttt{angry}. \texttt{sad}, \texttt{brooding}, and 
\texttt{lonely} do not form a simple ordering. Its displayed upper-left quadrant contains \texttt{playful}, \texttt{spiteful}, and 
\texttt{surprised}; the lower-left contains \texttt{loving}, \texttt{happy}, and \texttt{calm}; and the right-hand quadrants mix the 
remaining negative-labeled vectors with \texttt{confused}, \texttt{nervous}, and \texttt{disgusted}. Thus, the Gemma projections contain 
a partial valence-like separation, but PC2 
does not provide a stable, readily interpretable arousal separation in either set. A sign reversal between PCA plots would have 
no substantive interpretation because PCA component signs are arbitrary.\footnote{A related Hugging Face discussion of Gemma 4 E4B 
reports that an apparent PC2 change from separately fitted PCAs disappeared when the comparison used joint PCA and Procrustes analysis, 
illustrating why independently fitted PCA coordinates should not be compared directly: \url{https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E4B-it/discussions/8}.} 
The quadrant descriptions above are therefore visual diagnostics of the displayed plots, not fixed affective coordinates 
(\Cref{fig:gemma-pca-projection}).

\subsubsection{Cosine Similarity Heatmap}

Gemma 4 E2B's cosine matrices show the same broad pattern of local alignments and oppositions. In the nine-emotion set, 
\texttt{happy}--\texttt{calm} is 0.48, \texttt{afraid}--\texttt{angry} is 0.62, and \texttt{loving}--\texttt{afraid} 
is -0.66. In the 20-emotion set, \texttt{hopeful}--\texttt{inspired} is 0.82, \texttt{anxious}--\texttt{afraid} 
is 0.88, \texttt{afraid}--\texttt{angry} is 0.70, and \texttt{happy}--\texttt{calm} is 0.57. Lower absolute cosine 
similarity indicates weaker directional alignment, not greater representational overlap. The heatmaps therefore support 
descriptive clustering among several related labels, while substantial mixed and near-zero entries show that the geometry 
is not exhausted by a single affective partition (\Cref{fig:gemma-cos-sim9,fig:gemma-cos-sim20}).

\subsection{Cross-model summary and relation to prior replications}

Across both models, the most consistent geometric observation is a partial PC1 separation between several positive-labeled 
vectors and a group containing fear-, anger-, and anxiety-related vectors, together with corresponding local cosine-similarity 
families. This is qualitatively related to the community Gemma 4 E4B replication by Hsieh \cite{hsieh_replicating_2026}, which 
reports a valence-like PC1 and labels PC2 as arousal for a nine-emotion E4B analysis. Our nine-emotion E2B PCA has comparable 
first-two-component variance (58.3\% versus the E4B report's 60.5\%), but this is not a numerical replication: the model 
variant, corpus, vector construction, and neutral-control procedure differ. In particular, the present PC2 plots do not 
provide a stable enough ordering to support an arousal interpretation. The results therefore support a narrower conclusion: 
under the present extraction pipeline, both evaluated models exhibit structured directional geometry and intervention-sensitive 
token diagnostics, with partial but unvalidated alignment to affective labels.
